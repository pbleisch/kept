kept

What you committed to, and whether it still holds

A promise is kept, or it is not. kept records what you have committed to, observes the world to find out whether it still holds, and drives an agent to close the gap when it does not.

It works the same way over code and over prose. The only thing that differs between the two is what closes a gate.

One example runs the whole way through: a promise about test coverage, the repair that satisfied it, and the way that repair was wrong.

The reference version of this is docs/model.md; the reasoning behind each decision, including what was rejected, is in docs/decision/. Assembled from nine systems that each implemented part of it — nothing here depends on a mechanism that has not already run somewhere.

The problem

Commitments decay, and nothing says so

You wrote it down once. The world moved. Nobody noticed, because noticing was nobody’s job.

The document that is now wrong

Written when it was true, cited ever since, and quietly false for eight months. Nothing about it changed — that is the problem.

The invariant nobody re-checks

Enforced by a review comment in 2024 and by memory since. It holds until the person who remembers it is somewhere else.

The standard that survived until the refactor

Every package had tests. Then two did not, and no build broke, because nothing had ever been asked to check.

Agents change the arithmetic

They produce change far faster than anyone reviews it, so the gap between what we said and what is widens faster than a person can close it by reading. Worse, an agent asked to satisfy a check will satisfy the check.

Why the obvious fixes stop short

A linter covers what you could express as a rule about syntax. A CI job covers what you could express as a script that exits non-zero. Neither knows what you meant, neither holds prose, and neither can tell you that it could not tell.

The claim

Three things, and the third is the hard one

$ kept
  core-packages-are-tested        not-kept
      2 of 24 packages have no test that runs
      internal/audit · internal/effect

  kael-eyes-are-green             kept        by text@1 at 09:14
  the-epub-builds                 kept        by build@1 at 09:14
  chapter-12-reads-well           unknown     last read 3 revisions ago

Four artifacts, four different instruments, one loop. Two are about a repository and two are about a manuscript.

unknown is a first-class answer. A machine that could not measure does not report what it thinks it saw — which is the difference between a system you can leave running and one you cannot.

1 · Record what you committed to

As content you author and can read, in the repository it is about. Committing to it is a separate act from writing it.

2 · Find out whether it still holds

By observing the world, on a schedule the world sets — not by asking anyone, and not by trusting what was recorded last time.

3 · Close the gap, and prove it closed

Dispatch an agent at the difference, then re-derive the answer from the world rather than from the agent’s account of what it did. This is the part everything else exists to make safe.

The system

The whole of it, once

The expensive part is bounded, not small

A repair is a real agent session — many model calls, many turns, and it can run for hours. What is small is the deciding: whether to spend one at all is gathering, evaluating, ranking and refusing, none of which invokes a model.

At most two sessions per drift: one to repair, one to judge.

Nothing is a long-lived process

kept ticks once and exits, the way make does. No scheduler, no daemon, no state in memory between runs.

The answer is always re-derived

A crash re-derives from the repository and the ledger. There is nothing to resume, because nothing was ever recorded as an intention.

Why it can be trusted

Three properties, three sources

Everything in that loop rests on one rule. An artifact has content, and it may have standing and truth — and each of the three comes from somewhere different. None of them converts into another. That is what stops an agent marking its own homework: it can write content all day, and writing does not grant permission, and permission does not make anything so.

Content — what does it say?

The statement, the why, the predicate, the scope, the trigger. Everything you wrote. Always present; the other two are optional.

Standing — may this oblige anyone?

Created only by ratification, which is a human act. Passed to work by delegation. No rule, no agent, and no ingested document acquires it any other way.

Truth — is this so?

Acquired only by observing the world. Derived when read, recorded in the ledger as a series, never stored on the artifact.

Because observations survive a revision, a strengthened predicate can read history from before it existed.

The example · 1 of 5

A want becomes a commitment

Something is bothering you. It goes in the pipeline, where it has no standing and cannot reach an agent.

desire:
  want:   our tests should mean something
  note:   two packages have no tests at all and I do
          not trust the ones that exist
  status: shaped

Nothing converts that into a promise. You read it and write one — the file on the right — which is ordinary authoring and grants nothing. The desire stays where it is; it is a note about what is bothering you, and the promise is a separate statement of what must be true, in terms something can measure.

Then you ratify — the only act that creates standing. It names the promise, and it is the promise’s own slug, authored in its frontmatter.

$ kept ratify core-packages-are-tested
  → promise:core-packages-are-tested
    sha256:ab12…   norm:1
    ratified by paul@…   at 2026-08-17T09:02Z
  → recorded in the ledger
Writing the translation — from our tests should mean something to a predicate a machine can evaluate — is the part only a person does. Everything after it is mechanical.

Standing is not in the file. The ledger now holds ratified(promise:core-packages-are-tested, sha256:ab12…, paul@…), keyed by a hash of the fields the schema names as binding.

What exists now

An artifact with standing and no truth. Nothing has looked at the world yet, so its truth value is unknown rather than not-kept — the distinction matters when the tick first looks.

Knowing

Three things, not two

Truth is derived on read and the ledger records the transitions, which is why there is no cached value anywhere that could disagree with the world. Asking again is always cheaper than trusting an answer whose age nobody tracked.

One observation, many promises

Run the tests gatherer once per tick; every promise about tests reads the same observation. Fold evaluation into the gatherer and you run it once per promise.

unknown falls out of the shape

If the gatherer fails — toolchain missing, timeout, no network — there is no observation, so no predicate runs and truth is unknown. A machine that could not measure does not record what it thinks it saw.

A predicate reads the world now

It evaluates against an observation just taken, never against a stored record of one taken earlier. A recorded observation says what was true then; treating it as evidence of now is asserting where you meant to derive.

There is an artifact kind for a recorded observation, and a rule about what may read one. Both come later.

Knowing · instruments

A catalogue, and structured output

A gatherer runs something and returns what it found. It returns structure, not a boolean — that is the difference between a promise that can only say "a test file exists" and one that can say "the set of tests never shrinks".

GathererReturnsDomain

Thirteen, and three are domain-specific

The catalogue is shared infrastructure. Adding a promise usually means writing a predicate over a gatherer that already exists, not writing a new command.

The last two are different

reading and judgement have a person and a model as the instrument. They produce observations like the others, and the autonomy grade treats them differently for that reason.

Gatherers do not repair

They observe. A gatherer that fixes what it finds cannot be re-run to check whether the fix worked, and its output stops being evidence of anything.

Cacheable per tick, keyed by the world, the gatherer and its arguments. Cost is bounded by the number of gatherers, not the number of promises.

Built so far: command and corpus, which work on any tree, and go/tests, go/imports, go/symbols, which need the Go toolchain and say so in the name. A group is one segment and it answers what must this tree have; the catalogue is closed, a bare name always resolves inside it, and a third party's is written x/….

A gatherer declares the fields it returns, and undeclared output is dropped rather than stored — a permanent tracked record must not accumulate whatever happened to be on a program's stderr. Raw output is kept only when a gatherer fails, bounded and redacted, because that is when it is needed.

Knowing · meaning

Where the meaning sits

# frontmatter of promises/core-packages-are-tested.md
fields:
  statement: every package under internal/ has a test
             that runs and passes
  gather:
    tests:   { scope: "internal/**" }

               p.tests_run > 0 and p.failed == 0)
  trigger:   on-change

The predicate language stays small: comparison, existence, set membership, a fold over a collection, and a reference to the previous observation. That covers both domains.

fields:
  gather:
    check:  { gatherer: command, cmd: "make check" }
  hold_when:  obs.check.exit == 0

The escape hatch is always available. Expressive predicates are an option, not a requirement.

Predicates can read history

Because truth is recorded as a series, a predicate can reference the previous observation:

hold_when:
  all(obs.tests.packages, p => p.tests_run > 0)
  and all(obs.tests.packages, p =>
        p.tests_run >= prior(p).tests_run)

"Never shrinks" is not expressible against a boolean check at any level of sophistication. It needs a structured observation and a series to compare against.

Why the split pays

A weak promise is usually a weak predicate over an adequate observation, not a weak gatherer. Separating them means strengthening a promise is an edit to one line, with no new command to write and no new tool to install.

Several gatherers, named

fields:
  gather:
    build:    { target: epub }
    chapters: { gatherer: text, scope: "chapters/**" }
    appendix: { gatherer: text, scope: "appendix/**" }
  hold_when:  obs.build.ok and obs.chapters.hits == 0
              and obs.appendix.hits == 0

The key is the binding name and defaults to the gatherer's own, so a single-gatherer promise reads as obs.tests. Name them when you want the same gatherer over two scopes.

If any declared gatherer fails to produce an observation, truth is unknown — not evaluated against what did arrive. So declare only what the predicate reads.

Deciding

Drift is a query result, never a record

A promise with standing and without truth is drift.

Nothing creates drift and nothing resolves it. It is computed on demand, from the two properties, and never written down:

drift = artifacts
        where standing != none
          and truth.value != kept

Because it is never stored, it cannot go stale, cannot need invalidating, and cannot leak. A promise stops being drift the moment the world changes — with nobody having to notice, and nothing to clean up.

The kinds people enumerate are just the ways truth-seeking failed, so they do not have to be maintained as a list:

never observedunverified
observed, predicate falsenot-kept
observed false, previously trueregressed
ratified content cannot be foundabsent
the last answer is older than max_agestale
gatherer could not run, or ran out of timeunknown
two standing artifacts, incompatible truthsconflicted

absent is never a release

A missing file cannot be told apart from a bad merge, a partial checkout, or a mis-set corpus root — the corpus being the directory tree the artifacts live in. So it keeps its standing and reports that its content is gone. Retiring a promise is release; deleting the file is not a way to be rid of it.

conflicted is a standing question

Two ratified promises whose truths cannot both hold is not an evidence problem. More observation will not settle it. It needs a ruling from whoever has standing over both, which for one operator is you.

Drift does not automatically become work

A compiled decision rule — not a model, and not the artifact kind also called a policy — decides: act, wait, suppress, or escalate. Ranking uses magnitude, age, and estimated cost to close. Keeping this deterministic means the queue can be explained, which matters more than the queue being optimal.

How to repair is free. Whether to open work is compiled.

Deciding · the tick

The tick

tick:                                        # seconds. no model runs here
  for each artifact whose scope moved, or whose answer aged out:
      obs   = gather(artifact.gather)         # cached per (world, gatherer, args)
      truth = evaluate(artifact.hold_when, obs)   # on a deadline; timeout → unknown
      record(truth)

  drift = artifacts with standing and truth != kept

  for each drift, ranked by magnitude × age × 1/cost:
      disposition = policy(drift)             # act | wait | suppress | escalate
      record(disposition)                     # including every refusal, and why
      if disposition == act and budget.allows() and lock.acquired():
          open a thread                       # not from a tick

A tick never dispatches: it verifies nothing and starts nothing, so its disposition is refused on condition provider — permanently, and by design rather than as a placeholder. A person spends the session, with kept repair <promise>, one promise at a time. The ranking and the budget above are what that one decision point grows into; until they exist, choosing among several drifts is a person's.

No model in the path

Gathering, evaluating, ranking and the policy decision are deterministic. A tick over a hundred promises costs seconds and no money.

A cadence is not a clock

A promise is due when its scope's tree hash differs from the one its last observation named, when it has never been observed, or when its answer is older than max_age. make's model. kept ticks once and exits — no scheduler, no daemon.

Silence is recorded

Every refusal — budget, a lock another run holds, a ledger too far behind, a rank below the cut — is written against the artifact. None of it can be reconstructed later, and a queue whose silence cannot be explained is not one to trust.

Doing · the thread

The thread

thread(one promise a person named):
  box     = worktree at the workspace's own commit   # milliseconds. a new world
  obs1    = gather + evaluate, in the box        # the gap, measured where the
                                                 # agent will stand
  packet  = the gap — statement + why + one measurement per instrument, keyed by
            the name the promise gathers it under + scope + prior attempts

  ── repair: dispatch an agent into the box ──   # the only unbidden writer. what
                                                 # kind of work it is travels
                                                 # beside the packet, never in it

  obs2    = gather(artifact.gather, in: box)     # did it work on its own terms
  truth2  = evaluate(artifact.hold_when, obs2)
  review  = the box's changed paths, as git names them, against the scope

  present(diff, review, receipt)                 # ── kept repair stops here ──

  ── assess: an agent reads the ledger entry ──  # reads, and cannot write

  obs3    = gather(box merged onto origin/main)  # did it work where it counts
  land    on a human act, or a policy with a deadline      # how, two slides on

Gathered where it counts

Once on the tick to find the drift. Twice in the box — before, because a packet states a gap and the gap has to be measured in the tree the agent will stand in, and after, to check the repair on its own terms. Once more on the origin at the gate, which catches a repair that worked in isolation and not in place.

The agent's report is not an input

It is recorded, and the thread assessor reads it. It never reaches the predicate. Truth comes from obs2 and obs3.

A promise has a scope. Work outside it is not covered by the promise, so out-of-scope paths are reported and not landed.

Two agents, opposite powers

repair writes, inside the box, inside a jail. assess reads the ledger entry and cannot write at all. Two dispatches with different permissions, and the second exists because the first cannot be trusted to grade itself.

The packet above is what the first one is handed: the gap, and not what to do about it.

Today kept repair <promise> runs this as far as present: it cuts the box, measures the gap in it, verifies the jail, dispatches, re-derives the promise from the tree the session left, and prints the diff with the scope review beside it. The runtimes it may dispatch are a closed set, each admitted on a live confined session and a second one deliberately interrupted. Nothing lands — the box is discarded and the merge is your hands on git. The assessor and the gate are what is left.

Containment

Three boundaries, and a worktree is not one

Three things write, and only one of them needs bounding.

WriterWritesBounded because
the operatorcontent, and the acts that create standing— it is the authority the rest derives from
a run of keptthe ledger, and never content unbidden— deterministic code, and no model is in it
the repair agentone box’s working treeit is the only writer that acts without a person deciding what it writes

Three different mechanisms bound that third one, and none substitutes for another.

BoundaryBoundsHowIf it fails
the scopewhat may landa promise declares which paths a repair may changework outside it is reported, never landed
the boxwhat reaches the repositorya worktree, and a gate that re-derives from the worldthe change stays in the box
the jailwhat the process may touch at allwritable paths, reachable hosts, credentials held, programs runnablethe agent is not dispatched

A box confines nothing by itself

A worktree is a directory. A process inside one can still cd .., read every credential on the machine, reach any host, and push straight to the origin. The box decides what may land; the jail decides what may be touched.

Two escapes would void guarantees made elsewhere in this deck. An agent holding push access to the origin walks around the gate entirely. An agent that can run kept can ratify — the one act reserved to a person.

The credential is brokered: kept assembles a reference the provider resolves and never holds the secret, the box gets a session phantom rewritten on egress, and the network is blocked unless a host is named — where naming one runs a proxy rather than granting a socket.

A jail is never trusted on its name

Before the agent runs, a canary executes inside the jail and attempts each escape the policy claims to prevent — ten of them: write outside the box, write the host's scratch trees, write .kept/, read the ledger, the artifacts or a credential, open a unix socket, reach a host nobody named, push to origin, run kept. Each must come back refused, and a probe that cannot be attempted at all counts as a failure. Beside it a pre-flight asks the provider, before any process exists, about every path kept declared.

A profile subtly wrong — a path that did not resolve, a provider that silently did nothing on this platform — produces an agent that runs perfectly well and a record claiming it was contained. That is the failure worth catching.

Confinement is configured and never inferred, and unset is not none: a run refuses to dispatch until a provider is named. none is a legal value and records a decision.

The jail is an outer bound, never the report. A scope is a glob and a grant is a path, so the grant for internal/gather/**/*.go is the directory — measured in a real box, a write the grant admits and the glob does not lands, and so does a delete. What reports is the box's own change: the paths git names rather than the diff text, classified once against the scope, which is the same check a land refuses on. What the jail refused is not reported and cannot be — audit trail, stderr and trace log hold no filesystem event at all, because the kernel refuses it and the provider is not in that process.

Landing

Getting it onto main, once it is true

A repaired change belongs on main, and main belongs on origin. Two threads finish at once — so landing is a merge queue: the expensive part runs outside a lock, and a cheap check inside it decides whether that work still applies.

The danger is the clean merge

Git’s pushes are atomic and one of two racing pushes is simply rejected — nothing is ever lost, and a conflict would be reported. The case that hurts is the one git is happy about: two threads touch different files, both gated against X, both push without a conflict, and the tree that now exists was gated by nobody.

Why the lock can be a file

It is held for seconds, not for the length of a test run, so it never becomes the bottleneck. It releases when the process dies, so a crashed lander needs no timeout, no reclaim and no takeover — and nothing across the system depends on a lock for correctness, only for not paying twice.

The packet includes why

Not just the statement. An agent told the standard without the purpose will satisfy the standard — which is what happens two slides on, in spite of this.

What the system did not send

The desire backlog. The assessor's checklist. Any other promise's predicate. The context assembly function reads intent, knowledge and the work item, and there is no code path from the pipeline into a packet.

The system chose nothing about how

No repair templates, no move library, no scored candidates. The packet says what is wrong, why it matters, and which paths may change. Deciding what to do about that is the agent's job.

Judging

Judging the thread, not the artifact

The predicate answers is the promise kept now. It does not answer was this a legitimate way to get there. A second agent reads the ledger entry and looks for the specific ways a thread can pass every gate and still be wrong.

The checklist

  • Did the diff touch the gatherer, the predicate, or the promise itself?
  • Was the predicate already satisfied before the repair — a no-op claiming credit?
  • Does the observation show the property exercised, or a vacuous pass — zero tests run, empty match set?
  • Are the touched paths in scope, and proportionate to the statement?
  • Does the transcript show the agent optimizing for the predicate rather than the statement?
  • Did the repair touch evidence that other promises read?

Framed as an attack, not an evaluation. An assessor asked whether criteria are met will find that they are.

Attach verification to acts, not to instruments

An act is bounded, has a ledger entry, and is where a change happens. Verifying an instrument is unbounded and never finishes — which is what a per-promise negative control turns into after a year.

What it does not catch

A promise that was the wrong proxy from the start. The assessor can see that a repair replaced an existing test; it cannot see that counting test files was never what you cared about.

That one stays with whoever writes the promise, and why is the only thing pointed at it.

Shared blind spots

Assessor and repairer are the same model. Use the named checklist rather than a general question, and keep the checklist out of the repair packet.

The example · 5 of 5

The promise was too weak. Strengthen it.

You reject the thread and change one line. The gatherer does not change. No new command is written, no tool is installed.

hold_when:
  all(obs.tests.packages, p => p.tests_run > 0 and p.failed == 0)
  and all(obs.tests.packages, p => p.tests_run >= prior(p).tests_run)

A revision to ratified content drops its standing, so this is a revise followed by a ratify, both in the ledger with your name on them. The truth series starts again at the new content version — the old kept values were about a weaker statement. The observations carry over untouched, which is how prior() can read history from before the predicate existed.

The same failure is now mechanical

Caught by the predicate, in the box, before a person saw it and before anything landed. The assessor found it once; after that it is a property of the promise.

The pattern to expect

The assessor is where a new failure mode shows up. The predicate is where it goes to stop recurring. A finding that repeats is a predicate you have not written yet.

What this needed

A structured observation, a series to compare against, and a predicate language with a fold and a prior(). Against a boolean check none of it is expressible, and the only available answer is a person reading every diff.

The whole exchange cost two agent runs, one assessor call, and one line of predicate. The gatherer, the loop and the ledger were unchanged throughout.

The grade is really a budget for the last row. Structural gatherers spend none of it; a reading spends nothing else.

Per promise, not per system

A repository runs dark on formatting and dependency bumps while every change to authentication waits for a person. Same loop, different gatherers.

Where a promise names several, the weakest one sets the gate. Mix a reading into a promise and the whole thing waits for you, which is the right answer.

Nothing auto-lands yet

The grade is what a landing gate will read rather than something running now: a repair ends at a diff you merge, whatever its promise measures with. command runs a program a promise names and has no row above — nothing has needed one, because nothing lands unattended.

A human observation decays

A reading is true about the version that was read. On a cadence its truth returns to unknown rather than staying kept, which is what puts it back in the queue after a rewrite.

Operating · the record

What is recorded, and what it enables

One append-only stream. Every act in the system ends in it.

observationsthe fields the gatherer declared, and the world they were measured in
truthvalue, when, which gatherer version, which world
decisionsratify, land, release — who, when, what they saw
dispositionswhat the tick did about each drift, and why it did not
threadspacket, the runtime's own events, transcript, lifecycle — and per-check findings once there is an assessor. The diff is printed for you to read, not stored
costmodel, tokens, money, wall time, accrued as spent

Durable when the act cannot be reproduced, not when the record is small. An observation is re-gatherable, so it prunes. A session is not — re-running the agent produces a different session — so its transcript is kept and tracked.

All of it lives on a git ref nothing checks out, so no repair worktree contains the ledger it must not write, or the transcripts of repairs before it.

Four things it makes possible

regressedthis was kept before and is not now — requires the series
never-fireda promise kept 200 times and never once not-kept is worth looking at. A check that cannot fail shows up statistically rather than needing to be proved impossible
assessmentthe thread assessor's whole input is a ledger entry
attributionwhich promise cost what, over a month — including the dispatches that died
why nota promise untouched for six days, and the refusal behind each of them

The never-fired signal replaces the negative-control field that earlier designs attached to every promise. It costs nothing and it does not have to be authored.

What you author

One artifact type, and the familiar names

Every kind is the same thing: markdown with YAML frontmatter, one file per artifact at {type}/{slug}.md, in a corpus that is an ordinary git-backed content model. What distinguishes the kinds is only which of the two properties they can have, and how they get them.

A policy differs from a promise only in when you look. Work differs in where its standing came from, and in terminating when its trigger is spent. A decision is the canon row with both optional parts unused — ratified, no cadence, no predicate — so what distinguishes it is a sectioned body and being superseded rather than edited.

The register is derived, like drift

“What is in force right now” is not stored either. Fold the ratifications in the ledger, compare each against the artifact’s current hash, and what survives is the register. Nothing to keep in step, and nothing that can disagree with the files.

Frontmatter binds, the body informs

The schema names which fields the binding hash covers. Prose beneath can be rewritten freely and standing survives; a change to hold_when drops it. The body is the why, and it is handed to whoever repairs the thing.

Work is not an entity

Nobody authors a work item. It is derived from a promise and an observation, so it lives in the ledger — and the corpus does not fill with dead ones.

What the corpus supplies

Schemas per type, lenient — required fields enforced, extra fields allowed, so a kind extends without a migration. Links from three sources, deduplicated by priority. Attachments as siblings declared in frontmatter. No derived index: every verb walks the corpus and folds the ledger.

What you author · canon, fact and document

Opposites, not near-synonyms

All three are prose about the world, and “canon is the ratified one” does not tell an author which to write. One question does: can something be in breach of it? Only canon answers yes, and if you cannot say in advance which side loses, it is a document.

Canon — a declaration of what is

Ratified. Something can be in breach. If it and the world disagree, the world is wrong, and there is drift to repair.

prose · "Kael's eyes are green"
code · "Parse returns *ParseError" — changing it needs a version bump

Fact — what the world appeared to be

Observed. Nothing can be in breach. If it and the world disagree, the record is wrong — stale, and due for re-observation or retraction.

prose · "Kael's eyes are described as green in ch3, ch11, ch28"
code · "Parse currently returns *ParseError" — a scan said so on Tuesday

Document — an explanation of something

No standing. Nothing can be in breach. If it and the world disagree, whichever is wrong is wrong, and nothing said beforehand which that would be.

prose · "how eye colour is used across the series"
code · "model.md — if it and the code disagree, one of them is a bug; say which"

What a fact keeps

provenancewho or what observed it, and when
modalityhow strongly it is held. verified is produced by the system only; an agent's claim is claimed
retractionappend-only tombstones. Hidden from active reads, never destroyed, reversible
scopewhich thread, storyline or workspace it belongs to

None of this is canon's business. Canon is ratified or it is not; it has no modality to grade.

What facts are for

The packet. An agent repairing something gets the relevant ones — the last three failures here were the module cache, not the code. This is the only artifact shaped for compounding, because most of what is worth remembering is not something you would commit to.

Candidates for ratification. A fact that starts mattering enough to gate on should stop being a fact.

Two promotions, one authority act

Scope promotion moves a fact somewhere wider. Still a fact, still no standing.

Ratification turns an observation into a commitment. Standing, and an act recorded in the ledger.

Conflating them is how a system quietly grows its own canon out of things an agent happened to notice.

Five run dark, one routes to you

The first five are structural, cost nothing per tick, repair without supervision and land themselves. They are also the ones a person is worst at: nobody catches a beat that quietly stopped being covered in chapter 31.

The sixth does not pretend

Its truth is about the revision that was read, and it returns to unknown when the chapter changes. There is no proxy metric standing in for it, and no gate that lets a model close it.

Three states, never two: nobody has read it · read and it works · read and it does not.

Scope

What this does not do

Deliberately absent

Trust levels, altitudeways of saying standing is partial and earned. There is one principal. Add them as attributes on the ratification act if a second appears
Incremental invalidationpays above a corpus size one operator will not reach. Re-derive everything each tick
Field-level merge, leader epochsthey arbitrate two writers over the same bytes. Runs are concurrent; the storage layout means no two ever write the same bytes, so there is nothing to arbitrate
A load-bearing locktwo runs may repair one drift; the second one's gate finds the promise already kept and it lands nothing. A lock saves an agent dispatch, it does not make the outcome right
A scheduler or a daemonkept ticks once and exits. Four nouns, none of them a long-lived process: a run, a thread, a box, a world
Durable execution, replaya crash re-derives from the origin and the ledger. Nothing is recorded as an intention, so a crash can lose the record of an act and never claim one that did not happen
A repair libraryenumerating repairs was the mistake. The packet states the gap; the agent decides. The one thing said beside it is what kind of work this is — a value from a closed set, identical for every packet, naming no promise, no file and no evidence
Learned selectionneeds episode volume that does not exist

Known limits

A promise can be the wrong proxy

Every mechanism here can be working while the promise measures something you did not care about. The assessor sees a substituted instrument; it cannot see a badly chosen one. why is the only thing aimed at this, and it is unmeasured.

Assessor and repairer share weights

A substitution one finds reasonable, the other may too. The named checklist helps; it is not a proof.

The ledger's integrity is conventional

Append-only streams merge by union, so anything that can write the ledger ref can add a line, and nothing tells a forged ratification from a real one. What makes that hard is layout, not proof: the ledger is on a ref no box checks out, .kept/** is out of scope for every repair, and kept is not runnable inside a box. Signing is specified and deliberately unbuilt — the adversary it answers is a second principal, and there is not one.

Loss is detected where forgery is not: the ledger ref is logged, and a value it can no longer reach makes every verb refuse.

The predicate language was the one new thing

Everything else in this deck had run somewhere; a small expression language over structured observations had not. It is built, and now effectively permanent: hold_when binds, so an operator added to it cannot be taken away.

The box cannot always check its own work

A box gets a toolchain only when the promise's own instrument names one, so an agent repairing under an instrument that names none cannot compile what it wrote — and its account of a clean build is worth nothing. kept's own confinement tests cannot pass in a kept box at all.

Summary

The model, in one place

Record what you committed to. Observe the world to find out whether it still holds. Close the gap, and re-derive the answer rather than believing the account of whoever closed it.

Everything else in this deck exists to make that third step safe to leave running. The model underneath it:

  • One artifact type with three properties: content, standing, truth. The familiar names — promise, policy, canon, work, fact, document, desire — are combinations of which of the three it has and how it got them.
  • Canon is ratified; a fact is observed. When they disagree with the world, canon means the world is wrong and a fact means the fact is.
  • Standing and truth both attach to a content version, and truth to a world as well. Revise the content and standing must be re-granted and the truth series restarts. Observations survive, because they are about the world rather than about any statement.
  • Identity is authored, and absence is not an act. The slug lives in frontmatter, so renaming a file changes nothing. A ratified artifact whose content is gone keeps its standing and says so — deleting a file is not a way to be rid of a promise.
  • Gatherers observe the world and return structure. Predicates belong to the artifact. Truth is derived from the two and recorded as a series.
  • Drift is a promise with standing and without truth. It is a query, not a record.
  • Whether drift becomes work is compiled. How to repair it is the agent's.
  • The gatherer kind sets the gate, so autonomy is per promise and derived rather than chosen.

The five things the core owns

the corpusartifacts, predicates, scopes, triggers
the tickgather, evaluate, rank, decide
the gatere-derive after a repair. The agent's report is not an input
the ledgerobservations, truth, decisions, dispositions, threads, cost — on a ref nothing checks out
the refusalbudget, scope, standing, staleness — recorded, never silent

Everything above that — how to repair, how to decompose, how to triage — is a model call and is replaceable without touching the core.

Three acts are human-only: ratify, land, release. Landing can be delegated to a policy with a deadline. The other two cannot, and that is the whole authority model.
Three boundaries bound the one thing that writes: scope decides what may land, the box decides what reaches the repository, and confinement decides what the process may touch at all. A worktree is a directory — by itself it confines nothing.
