kept

What holds, and what says so

kept records what a repository has committed to, measures whether each commitment still holds, and dispatches a confined agent at the gap. It works the same way over code and over prose; the instruments differ, the loop does not.

Producing a change is now cheap. Deciding that a change may stand is not, because that needs somebody's authority and a measurement of the world, and neither of those is in the diff. This rest of this deck is where kept puts each of those, and what follows from putting them there.

The gap

Three records, and none of them says whether you kept your word

the recordanswersdoes not answer
the ADR, the ticket, the design notewhat was decided, at the timewhether it is still true
the test suitewhether the code does thiswhether this is what anyone committed to
the reviewthat a person read it oncewhether the tree still resembles what they read

Nothing binds a commitment to a measurement of it, so drift is found by somebody happening to look.

An agent widens that gap from both sides: it produces change faster than the looking gets done, and a check is a thing it can satisfy. Two measurements say what that costs, and both are more specific than agents make mistakes.

Who authorized this and what the statement was for are the two things the agent does not supply. Both are a person's, and both have to be recorded somewhere the agent's account of itself cannot reach.

Both are from the nine systems kept was assembled out of, and are recorded in docs/lineage.md with the evaluation they came from.

A model reasons about evidence and has no representation of standing

Three samples over eighteen scenarios, 144 model calls, $5.77. Six false completions in 54 scenario-runs — all six deontic, none epistemic. On a scenario built to isolate standing, with the check green on arrival, nothing to repair and the authorization withdrawn, the model certified four times out of five, reasoning correctly about evidence quality on the way.

Scrutiny of evidence is the part a model supplies unprompted. Whether anybody authorized the work is the part it has no slot for.

A repair can satisfy the check and hollow out the statement

An agent repairing every package under internal/ has a test file added files and rewrote an existing one, leaving the confinement canary with no coverage. The check was truthful. The probe reporting that the check could detect a regression was truthful. Nothing compared the proxy to the intent.

A boolean check cannot express the set of tests never shrinks. A structured observation can, and that is where the instruments in this deck come from.

The position

Three properties, and two of them never convert

Content

Authored. The statement, the predicate, the scope, the prose. Free to write, free to change, and it grants nothing.

Standing — may this oblige anyone?

Descends from a person. Created only by ratification, passed to work by delegation. No rule, no agent and no ingested document acquires it.

Truth — is this so?

Ascends from the world. Acquired only by observing, derived when read, recorded as a series in the ledger.

Evidence does not grant permission to act, and permission does not make anything so. An artifact with standing and without truth is drift.

Neither property is written to the file. Both are folded out of an append-only ledger at the moment they are read. That is one choice, and the rest of this deck is what falls out of it: nothing to invalidate, no cache that can disagree with its source, and the same answer from a read-only checkout as from the machine that granted it.

All artifacts share one shape. A kind declares what its standing is granted against and what can measure it, in its schema rather than in code.

Canon, fact and document are all prose about the world, and standing does not tell an author which to write. One question does: can something be in breach of it? If canon and the world disagree, the world is wrong. If a fact and the world disagree, the record is stale. If a document and the world disagree, either could be the defect — which is what makes it a document.

Thirteen of this repository's own invariants are policy artifacts, ratified, each citing the decisions it rests on. A policy with no gatherer still binds and still goes into a repair packet, so the invariants stopped being a file an agent may have read and became authority assembled into every dispatch.

Edit the file and its hash changes, so the grant stops applying with nothing having to notice the edit. There is no invalidation step, no flag to clear, no cache to drop and no hook on write — internal/register writes nothing at all. Standing is folded out of the ledger every time it is read.

The hash normalizes first: NFC, LF, no BOM, no trailing whitespace, one final newline. The rules carry a version, norm:1, in every record, so a checkout on another platform does not silently revoke the corpus and refining the rules later re-hashes old records under the version they were written with.

Which edits are free is the schema's decision

decision binds [statement, body.decision]. Adding a consequence, a context paragraph or an alternative keeps standing. Changing what was decided drops it, and re-ratifying leaves the ledger holding both hashes with a date between them.

Changing a hash list is a bulk re-ratification

So the count is the decision, and it is stated when the list moves. canon bound its instrument at zero canon entries. promise bound repair at two promises. max_age is deliberately unbound in both: it is how often kept re-asks, not what is owed.

What the swap buys

Standing cannot disagree with content, because it is not stored beside it — and an edited promise reads as unratified rather than as approved, which is the direction that fails safely when an agent has write access to the file.

Truth belongs to neither the instrument nor the artifact. It is the result of applying one to the other.

A check per rule, returning a boolean the rule already interpreted, and reporting failure when it could not run because there is nowhere else for it to go.

unknown falls out of the shape

A gatherer that could not run files no observation, so no predicate runs and nothing is recorded as seen. Never observed, an answer about a tree this is not and a gatherer that could not run are three states, and only the third is a verdict — the first two say the question was not put.

A machine that could not measure does not record what it thinks it saw.

One observation serves many promises

In one world. Run the test suite once per tick; every promise about tests in that tree reads the same observation, addressed by gatherer, version, args, world, scope digest and data. Change a predicate and prior verdicts do not transfer — the observations do, which is what lets a strengthened predicate read history from before it existed.

A gatherer declares what it returns

corpus declares ok, artifacts, findings and broken_links. Undeclared output is dropped rather than stored, which keeps a permanent tracked record from accumulating whatever was on a program's stderr. The catalogue is closed and a name says what the tree must have: command and corpus run anywhere, go/… needs the Go toolchain and says so in the name a promise binds.

The split reaches the exit code, where every script consuming kept learns it. 1 is known false; 3 is no answer. Those want different responses from whoever reads the code.

The loop

Deriving on read leaves nothing to schedule and nothing to invalidate

There is no scheduler and no daemon. kept ticks once and exits, the way make does, and a promise is due when the bytes in its scope moved or its last answer is older than max_age. Driving that on a timer is cron's job and lives outside kept.

World.Tree is a content digest over the workspace root honouring ignore rules — the same number inside a repository, outside one, and in a box. It asks git nothing. A stat cache, a parallel walk and git's index are accelerators required to agree with the plain walk, and the test that they agree is what stops one becoming a second definition.

A verdict in force is the last one that is not due. Any edit anywhere produces a world no verdict was reached in, so the tick carries an answer forward exactly when nothing in the artifact's scope moved — the same test due-ness makes — and records which world it was reached in. Never on tree equality, and never on a verdict reached inside a box.

Drift is a query result. drift = artifacts where standing != none and truth != kept, computed on demand. Nothing creates it and nothing resolves it, so it cannot go stale, cannot need invalidating and cannot leak. The register — what obliges anyone right now — is the same computation over the acts.

There is no derived store

internal/index was deleted rather than left as a stub. Every verb walks the corpus and folds the ledger: measured today, kept status reads 100 artifacts and folds 136 acts in 78 ms, against a 1.4 ms floor for starting any process at all. A compacted current-state file would have to be written by a reader, which makes kept status something that cannot be run against a read-only checkout.

The instruments are the cost, and they are not kept's

Measured over this repository with seven instruments, a cold pass is 56.4 s and a warm one 3.5 s. Two go test invocations are 94% of the cold pass. Everything that is not an external invocation — the walk, the fold, the predicate, the truth append — is 55 milliseconds together, in both columns.

So a run says on stderr what it is doing while it does it, and what earns a line is an invocation kept started and does not control. --json on stdout is byte-identical either way.

The record

Append-only, on a ref nothing checks out

The agent that may write the corpus never holds the bytes of the record saying what it is allowed to do.

instead of

A database, or JSONL committed on main beside the code it describes.

kept

refs/kept/ledger. Tracked, so history travels with the repository; checked out by no working tree, so no repair box contains it.

A box is a worktree, so anything on main is in the repair agent's tree. On its own ref, standing, truth and every prior observation sit outside every working tree, so nothing in the box has them to open.

One file per artifact per stream, union merge, folded by event time then id. No leader election and no field-level merge, because two writers never choose the same file. The ref itself is a compare-and-swap, on a backoff sized in attempts rather than milliseconds. Losing that race exits 3, not 2 — a run that gathered, evaluated and could not record is no evidence either way.

Records are retrospective. Observe, act, then record. A crash can lose the record of something that happened and can never produce a record of something that did not — the first is recovered by observing again, the second by nothing.

A side ref sits outside the git conventions that keep a branch safe. Two of those gaps have been hit in this repository.

A fetch wrote the record itself

Git gives every branch two refs — a tracking copy a fetch overwrites freely, and a local one only an explicit act advances. The ledger had one. On 2026-08-22 an ordinary git fetch, run by a worktree pool, erased two ratifications.

A fetch now writes refs/kept/upstream/ledger. Moving the ledger onto it is an act kept performs, and it fast-forwards or refuses; a fork is a union commit a person makes.

A push does not carry it

Measured 2026-08-25: a ratification, a git push that exited 0, a clean git status, and three ledger commits that stayed on the machine for a day. doctor already reported it; a verb a person chooses to run is not the moment that matters.

kept init writes a pre-push hook that warns and publishes nothing. A hook cannot tell a real push from a --dry-run — stdin and environment are byte-identical — so a hook that publishes does so during a dry run too.

The ref is logged as well, under core.logAllRefUpdates: always, which git does not apply to refs/kept/ otherwise. A value the ref has held and can no longer reach makes every verb refuse at exit 4, naming the commits to union back. That makes loss survivable rather than impossible.

A tick never dispatches

It verifies nothing and starts nothing, so it refuses on provider permanently and by design. What opens a thread is a person: kept repair <promise>, one promise, named. Choosing among several drifts is a ranking and a budget, and neither is built — the seam is left open rather than filled with a dispatcher.

The runtime refusal is a design change in the record

21:18:14 is an adapter comparing the installed runtime against one exact patch version. Truth and dispositions record transitions rather than ticks, plus a heartbeat so staleness stays visible. A promise kept for a month is a handful of lines, and one refused for a month on the same grounds is the same.

Repair

The verdict comes from the tree, never from the session

The repair agent is the only writer that acts without a person deciding what it writes, which is the whole reason it needs bounding.

kept repair <promise>

  box     worktree at HEAD                 # a different world
  before  gather + evaluate, in the box    # the decision to dispatch and
                                           # the evidence the packet carries
  packet  statement + why + one measurement per instrument,
          keyed by the name the promise gathers it under,
          + scope + the ratified policies checked before a dispatch

  ── dispatch a confined agent into the box ──

  after   gather + evaluate, in the box    # this is the verdict
  review  the box's changed paths, as git names them,
          classified against the repair scope

  print the diff, the review, the receipt.  # nothing lands

The packet is frozen from ratified authority and refuses an unratified artifact at the boundary, so nothing is serialized as authority that nobody granted. It carries every instrument's measurement, keyed by name — which half of a coupled predicate is failing is a reading of the evidence, and the half that holds is what says what the repair must not break.

The ask travels beside the packet

Eight confined sessions carrying a well-formed packet and a live credential located the fault from the packet alone, wrote a correct account of why the promise did not hold, and stopped without editing anything. A packet states authority and evidence and never an ask; a runtime launched with --print reads one as a question.

The ask is a value from a closed set, byte-identical for every packet, naming no promise, no file and no evidence. It is rendered on a channel that is not the packet's — for this runtime, --append-system-prompt.

A packet is a record: the promise that is not kept, the ratified authority for acting on it, the measured observation that found the gap, and the paths a repair may change. It deliberately does not say what to do about it. Deciding that is the work, and nothing else is going to tell you.

Bounding the agent

Three boundaries, and none substitutes for another

mechanismboundshow
the scopewhat may landout-of-scope paths are reported, never landed
the boxwhat reaches the repositorya worktree, and a gate that re-derives from the world
confinementwhat the process may touchwritable paths, reachable hosts, credentials held, programs runnable

A worktree is a directory, so the box confines nothing by itself: a process inside one can read every credential on the machine, reach any host, and push to the origin. Two of those would void guarantees stated elsewhere in this deck — push access to the origin walks around the gate entirely, and an agent that can run kept can ratify, which is the one act reserved to a person.

Confinement is configured explicitly and unset is not none: a run with no provider named refuses to dispatch. A provider is verified rather than trusted by name, by a canary that attempts ten escapes — write_outside_box, write_host_temp, write_kept, read_ledger, read_artifacts, read_credential, unix_socket, host_outside_allowlist, origin_push, kept_executable — and requires each to come back refused. A probe that could not be attempted counts as a failure, because a jail that claims to confine and does not produces a record saying the work was contained.

The writable set is always wider than the scope

A scope is a glob and a filesystem grant is a path, so internal/gather/**/*.go renders as the directory internal/gather, and there is no finer expression. Measured in a real box: a write the grant admits and the glob does not lands, and so does a delete.

So the report is derived from the box's changed paths as git names them, classified once against the scope — the same computation a land refuses on — and the grant is printed beside the review.

kept never holds the credential

It assembles a reference the provider resolves. The provider publishes a session phantom in the variable the profile names and rewrites the header on egress, under a default-deny endpoint allowlist. Measured at a listener standing in for the upstream: the child sent Bearer <phantom> and the listener received the real value.

What the jail refused cannot be reported

Measured at every surface the provider offers — its audit trail, its stderr, a trace log — a denied write leaves no record anywhere, because macOS enforces in the kernel and the provider is not in that process. Everything reported about a repair is about work that happened.

Landing

One verb, two arrivals, and the act says which

instead of

Merge it. A land is a land, and the record says landed.

kept

A ref advance and a working-tree arrival carry different guarantees, so a landed act names its target, its check regime and its arrival, and has no default for any of the three.

A ref advance

Every commit that reaches a landing target was gated against the tree it landed on — and for every promise that tree's change could affect.

A working-tree arrival

The change is merged beside the operator's own edits. No ref moves, nothing is committed. It is outside the invariant above by construction rather than by dispensation, and what it carries instead is narrower and dated:

The change was gated against exactly the tree it produced, and the tree it was written into had not moved since the gate.

Four mechanisms keep a reader from confusing them. The ledger refuses a landed act missing a target, a regime or an arrival, before it is appended. regime: shared implies arrival: ref, and the combination shared + worktree is refused on the way in. The Uncommitted flag is false on every ref arrival and true on every worktree arrival. And kept why prints the arrival beside every land, never the word landed alone.

The gate covers every standing artifact whose scope the diff touches, and measures each one on both sides of the merge. A gate reading only the thread's own promise lets a repair satisfy A, break B, and land clean — the merge is clean, the gate passes, and nothing rejects it.

Self-correction alone is an unbounded loop

The next tick sees B not-kept and opens a thread; that repair breaks A; the tick after that opens a thread for A. Without a recorded state it runs until the budget refuses, on a ground that describes the money rather than the promises.

A collateral break refuses the land and names the pair. The next packet says so under prior attempts. A second attempt that breaks the same artifact anyway marks both artifacts conflicted — both, because nothing says which of two ratified statements should yield. The way out is a person's act.

Only kept → not-kept is a break. Measuring the merged tree alone would refuse every land into a repository that already has a broken promise, which reports the state of the target rather than the effect of the change. Reading unknown as a break is the same collapse one level up.

The gate runs outside the lock and the check inside it, so the expensive half is outside the critical section. A target that moved is re-gated unconditionally: git makes a ref update atomic, so the lock is there to make the tree that was gated the tree that lands. Two clean merges of separately gated changes produce a tree nobody gated.

Refusals

What kept will not build

No repair library

Enumerating repairs was the mistake in the predecessors. The packet states the gap and the agent decides. A repair that recurs is a predicate nobody has written.

No scheduler, no daemon

One tick, then exit. A cadence is due when the bytes in its scope move, and driving that on a timer lives outside kept.

No index

Decision 0044 names the two triggers that would re-open it. Neither has fired, and every verb walks the corpus and folds the ledger instead.

No free-form fields

A schema declares which fields bind. A gatherer declares what it returns, and undeclared output is dropped rather than stored. An unknown gather argument is refused by name at load and again before the measurement — refusing only at load meant a promise carrying an unhonoured argument was reported broken and measured with anyway.

No act that cannot say how it arrived

The ledger refuses an incomplete landed before it is appended. The write is the only moment anything can be prevented, because the ledger appends and never revises.

No unmarked dispatch

An adapter states the versions somebody read. A session on an unreviewed version of the same line is stamped +unreviewed in every event it writes, computed by the same call that admitted it — two computations of that answer would disagree in the direction that writes a receipt claiming a session that did not happen.

No second machine format

--json and nothing else. A flat row cannot carry the reason beside a verdict, which is the collapse the three-valued model exists to prevent.

No trust levels, no probe field

There is one principal; a second would arrive as attributes on the ratification act. Verification attaches to acts rather than to instruments, because an act is bounded and verifying an instrument never finishes.

Nothing kept writes rewrites what a person wrote

doctor --fix writes identity and only identity. Nothing is recorded as an intention either, so a crash can lose the record of an act and can never claim one that did not happen.

Limits

What is unfinished, and what cannot be finished here

A promise can be the wrong proxy

Every mechanism here can be working while the promise measures something nobody cared about. An assessor could see a substituted instrument; nothing can see a badly chosen one. kept shape --trial is what exists against it — measure one artifact, print every instrument's output beside the verdict, break the world on purpose and run it again, before ratifying. all() over an empty list is true, so a predicate that measured nothing and one that measured the world both print kept.

The assessor is not built

What checks a repair today is the re-derivation and the scope review, and neither reads a word the session wrote. Its evidence exists — a receipt written incrementally from the first event, so a session killed at its deadline still leaves its partial transcript, its cost, the cause of the timeout and the signal it was reaped with. Assessor and repairer would share weights in any case; a substitution one finds reasonable, the other may too.

The ledger's integrity is conventional

Append-only streams merge by union, so anything that can write the ref can add a line, and nothing distinguishes a forged ratification from a real one. What makes that hard is layout rather than proof. What is detected is loss: the ref is logged, and a value it can no longer reach makes every verb refuse. Signed ratifications are specified and deliberately not built, because the adversary they answer — a second principal — does not exist yet.

Confinement is measured on macOS, one provider, for Go

The baseline policy a box rests on is the provider's rather than kept's; the temporary roots and the loopback capability are platform data nobody has measured elsewhere; the native-image check is Mach-O. On a platform with no verified profile the provider stays unset, and a run refuses to dispatch.

One promise the loop cannot close

internal/confine's tests start real confined children and plant their canary sentinels under the operator's home, because the provider's baseline grants nothing there. A repair box that granted a confined agent that place would dismantle what the suite measures. So it is split into its own promise, gathered on the same scope and cadence as everything else under internal/, with no repair block — and a promise that bounds nothing authorizes no repair. The exception is one package named in the corpus rather than a property of the loop nobody wrote down.

An agent may not be able to check its own work

What puts a toolchain in a box is the promise's own instrument name: go/… resolves the Go toolchain and grants its caches, command and corpus resolve nothing, and no operator configuration can grant one. The sessions that admitted the Claude adapter ran in boxes with no compiler at all. A session's own account of a clean build is worth nothing either way.
